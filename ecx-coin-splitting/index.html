How to Split Your ECX from BTC Safely

Learn how to split your ECX from BTC safely, why a Bitcoin transaction could be replayed on the ECX chain, and what to verify before moving BTC. This guide explains the risks in plain English and links to the official instructions; it does not construct transactions or handle funds.

Last checked: 9 October 2026. The ECX project currently describes mainnet as planned for around 31 October 2026 at Bitcoin block 973,728. Dates and software readiness can change, so confirm the latest official timeline before acting.

Important: pause before moving pre-fork BTC.

Bitcoin and a forked chain can share transaction history. Depending on the networks' transaction rules and replay protection, a transaction intended for one chain may also be valid on the other. Do not assume your BTC and ECX are separated automatically. Check the latest official instructions before making a transaction.

What does “splitting ECX” mean?

Coin splitting is the process of separating assets on two networks that share some transaction history. After a fork, the BTC you hold remains on the Bitcoin network; any corresponding fork-coin balance follows the fork's rules.

Replay protection is designed to prevent a transaction made on one chain from being accepted and replayed on another. The exact protections and steps depend on the network and the tools being used. Read the official ECX documentation for the current implementation rather than relying on a generic wallet tutorial.

What the current official ECX documentation says: its described opt-in replay-protection marker uses nLockTime = 499999999 on an ECX transaction, with at least one input sequence below 0xffffffff so Bitcoin enforces the locktime while ECX treats the transaction as final. This is a technical explanation, not a transaction recipe: a safe split depends on the exact wallet, inputs, network rules, confirmations and current official procedure. Do not hand-build or sign a transaction based on this summary alone. See the official coin-splitting guide for the current details.

Replay protection is opt-in according to the current project guidance; do not assume every transaction is protected automatically. A successful split can also be affected by chain reorganisations, so follow the official confirmation and verification procedure rather than relying on a generic number of confirmations.

What does a successful split protect?

A successful split protects only the spendable outputs included in that split. It does not automatically separate every account or every coin in your wallet. Other accounts, omitted outputs and coins received later may still be shared between the networks and may need attention. Changing the destination address alone does not provide replay protection. Follow the current official splitting procedure for your wallet and network, follow the applicable release confirmation guidance, and verify the result on both chains before making independent transactions.

Who may be eligible for ECX?

Eligibility depends on the official snapshot rules, the amount and type of BTC held at the relevant time, and how your coins are held. If your BTC is on an exchange or with a custodian, that provider controls the keys and decides whether it supports the fork and how it handles any resulting assets.

Do not assume that every exchange will credit ECX, or that assets held in every wallet or contract will qualify. Confirm the rules directly with the official project and your provider.

For self-custody, keep control of your recovery phrase and verify that your wallet supports the relevant ECX network and splitting procedure. For exchange-held BTC, ask the exchange about snapshot eligibility, ECX crediting and withdrawals; do not send coins to a personal wallet just because an unofficial post recommends it.

How to split ECX safely: checks before moving BTC

The safest useful answer is not a one-size-fits-all transaction sequence. The official procedure and supported software are what matter. Use this checklist to prepare without risking funds:

  1. Confirm custody.
    Determine whether you control your own Bitcoin keys or whether an exchange or other custodian holds them.
  2. Read the latest official splitting guide.
    Fork procedures, supported wallets and network details can change. Use the official documentation linked below.
  3. Verify software and network.
    Only use software obtained from verified official sources. Confirm that it supports the relevant network and current splitting procedure.
  4. Follow the official process carefully.
    Do not improvise transactions or copy instructions from unverified social posts. If the procedure is unclear, pause and seek trusted technical help.
  5. Verify before sending funds.
    Check the transaction details, network and destination independently before confirming any transfer.

Frequently asked questions about ECX splitting

Can I move my BTC before splitting?

Do not assume it is safe. Replay risk depends on the networks and their protections. Read the current official coin-splitting instructions before moving BTC associated with the fork.

Does SplitYourECX.com split my coins for me?

No. This is an educational website only. It does not connect to wallets, build transactions or hold funds. The actual procedure is described by the official project.

What if my BTC is held on an exchange?

Check the exchange's official fork policy. If the exchange controls the keys, you cannot independently run a self-custody splitting procedure on those funds.

Can someone split my coins if I give them my seed phrase?

Never give your seed phrase to another person or website. Anyone with your seed phrase may be able to take your funds. Use verified documentation and keep your recovery phrase private.

What is Bitcoin–ECX transaction replay?

Replay happens when a transaction intended for one chain is also valid on another chain with compatible transaction rules. If the assets have not been safely separated, a BTC spend could potentially move the corresponding ECX too. Check the current official splitting procedure before moving pre-fork coins.

When is the ECX Bitcoin fork?

As checked on 9 October 2026, the official project describes mainnet as planned for around 31 October 2026 at Bitcoin block 973,728. This is a target, not a guarantee; check the official timeline for changes.

Does 1 BTC mean 1 ECX, and does that mean equal value?

The official project describes a 1:1 snapshot balance for eligible BTC at the mainnet snapshot. That does not guarantee equal market prices, liquidity, exchange support, or that every custody arrangement is eligible. Verify the current snapshot and custody rules with official sources.

What if I hold BTC on an exchange?

The exchange controls the private keys and decides whether it supports the fork and credits ECX. Check its official announcement and withdrawal policy directly. Support is not guaranteed.

Can I use the same wallet or address for BTC and ECX?

Do not assume address similarity means a wallet supports both networks or that transactions are replay-protected. Confirm network selection, software support and the current official splitting procedure before sending funds.
